# Curriculum and evidence map

## 1. Why can mean several different questions

Distinguish mechanism, development, evolutionary history and proposed function in a behavioural explanation.

- Different why questions can complement each other.
- Tinbergen distinguished four questions.
- A function claim needs testing.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: History of psychology](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/1-2-history-of-psychology); [Tinbergen (1963): On aims and methods of Ethology](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1963.tb01161.x); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Evolutionary Psychology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evolutionary-psychology/)

## 2. Selection has no foresight

Explain natural selection using heritable variation and differential reproduction without implying progress or purpose.

- Selection changes populations over generations.
- Fitness is not health, virtue or happiness.
- Change has more than one source.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Human genetics](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-1-human-genetics); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Evolutionary Psychology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evolutionary-psychology/); [Gould, Lewontin (1979): The spandrels of San Marco and the Panglossian paradigm: a critique of the adaptationist programme](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1979.0086)

## 3. A useful feature is not automatically an adaptation

Compare adaptation and by-product hypotheses and identify evidence needed for a mismatch claim.

- Several origins can fit a present feature.
- Design claims need discriminating predictions.
- Mismatch needs a specified contrast.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: History of psychology](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/1-2-history-of-psychology); [Gould, Lewontin (1979): The spandrels of San Marco and the Panglossian paradigm: a critique of the adaptationist programme](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1979.0086); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Evolutionary Psychology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evolutionary-psychology/)

## 4. Genes and learning do not take turns

Explain how evolved capacities develop through experience without assigning fixed genetic percentages to individuals.

- Development constructs the phenotype.
- Heritability is a population statement.
- Learning can be part of an evolved solution.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Human genetics](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-1-human-genetics); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Evolutionary Psychology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evolutionary-psychology/)

## 5. Helping relatives has more than one explanation

Explain inclusive fitness without equating biological relatedness with conscious calculation or moral obligation.

- Fitness consequences can extend through relatives.
- Hamilton’s rule is conditional.
- Non-kin care is not a contradiction.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Prosocial behavior](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/12-7-prosocial-behavior); [Hamilton (1964): The genetical evolution of social behaviour. I](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-5193(64)90038-4); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Biological Altruism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/altruism-biological/)

## 6. Cooperation can depend on the game

Distinguish direct reciprocity, indirect reciprocity and institutional support for cooperation.

- Repeated interaction can change incentives.
- Reputation can affect a wider network.
- Institutions change the available relations.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Prosocial behavior](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/12-7-prosocial-behavior); [Trivers (1971): The Evolution of Reciprocal Altruism](https://doi.org/10.1086/406755); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Biological Altruism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/altruism-biological/)

## 7. Relationships are not an ancestral instruction manual

Distinguish evolutionary models of reproduction from fixed predictions about modern individuals or ethical rules.

- Sexual selection concerns reproductive differences.
- Investment and constraints vary.
- A difference needs a well-designed comparison.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Sexual behavior](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/10-3-sexual-behavior); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Evolutionary Psychology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evolutionary-psychology/); [Henrich, Heine, Norenzayan (2010): The weirdest people in the world?](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X0999152X)

## 8. Culture also changes across generations

Distinguish cultural transmission from genetic inheritance and explain how the processes can interact.

- Information can spread socially.
- Copying can follow different cues.
- Genes and culture can influence each other.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Observational learning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-4-observational-learning-modeling); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Evolutionary Psychology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evolutionary-psychology/); [Henrich, Heine, Norenzayan (2010): The weirdest people in the world?](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X0999152X)

## 9. A universal claim needs varied evidence

Evaluate evolutionary hypotheses using diverse samples, competing predictions and replication.

- Sampling limits the conclusion.
- State a risky prediction in advance.
- Corroboration is more than a matching story.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Analyzing findings](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/2-3-analyzing-findings); [Henrich, Heine, Norenzayan (2010): The weirdest people in the world?](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X0999152X); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Evolutionary Psychology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evolutionary-psychology/)

## 10. An explanation is not an instruction

Build a testable evolutionary account while separating scientific description from ethical recommendation.

- Natural does not mean good or inevitable.
- Use a bounded evolutionary account.
- Practical decisions need current evidence.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Ethics](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/2-4-ethics); [Tinbergen (1963): On aims and methods of Ethology](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1963.tb01161.x); [Gould, Lewontin (1979): The spandrels of San Marco and the Panglossian paradigm: a critique of the adaptationist programme](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1979.0086); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Evolutionary Psychology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evolutionary-psychology/)

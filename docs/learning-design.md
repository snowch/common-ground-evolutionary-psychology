# Learning design

Audience: interested adult beginners who want to interpret human behaviour using evidence. The gap is between attaching an intuitive label and explaining a case with accurate concepts, alternatives and limits. Delivery is a standalone, accessible, learner-paced GitHub Pages course, not an LMS or certification.

The established café visual identity uses Libre Baskerville headings, Arimo body text, pale blue backgrounds, navy text, teal interactions and restrained chapter accents. Reading and controls precede the supporting figure in the stacked layout. The primary learning path remains clear when research disclosures stay closed.

Each chapter has a first decision, three separate narrated explanations, a visible evidence check, a new written transfer case, unaided retrieval and low-stakes field practice. The final case integrates chapters. Page visits indicate navigation, not mastery. No learner pilot or independent academic review has been conducted.

## 1. Why can mean several different questions

Objective: Distinguish mechanism, development, evolutionary history and proposed function in a behavioural explanation.

Initial decision: Which distinction helps the discussion?

Transfer: A researcher explains cooperation by describing how a child learned local turn-taking practices. What kind of explanation is this?

Field application: Use a fictional act of helping to write one mechanism question, one development question and one evolutionary question.

## 2. Selection has no foresight

Objective: Explain natural selection using heritable variation and differential reproduction without implying progress or purpose.

Initial decision: What corrects the visitor’s explanation?

Transfer: A widespread characteristic is observed, but no evidence about its origin or function is provided. Which conclusion follows?

Field application: Rewrite a fictional nature-wanted-it claim using variation, inheritance and conditions, with no moral conclusion.

## 3. A useful feature is not automatically an adaptation

Objective: Compare adaptation and by-product hypotheses and identify evidence needed for a mismatch claim.

Initial decision: What is missing from Leo’s music claim?

Transfer: A headline attributes a modern difficulty to evolutionary mismatch. What should the researcher specify?

Field application: Choose a fictional adaptation headline. Write one alternative account and one result that would weaken the headline.

## 4. Genes and learning do not take turns

Objective: Explain how evolved capacities develop through experience without assigning fixed genetic percentages to individuals.

Initial decision: Which statement best corrects both visitors?

Transfer: A study reports heritability for a measured trait in one population. What does the estimate establish?

Field application: Rewrite a fictional nature-versus-nurture argument as a question about interacting developmental processes.

## 5. Helping relatives has more than one explanation

Objective: Explain inclusive fitness without equating biological relatedness with conscious calculation or moral obligation.

Initial decision: What is wrong with Leo’s conclusion?

Transfer: A commentator applies Hamilton’s rule to a family favour using only its monetary cost. What needs clarification?

Field application: Use a fictional act of family help to write a proximate account and a separate evolutionary question, without assigning a motive.

## 6. Cooperation can depend on the game

Objective: Distinguish direct reciprocity, indirect reciprocity and institutional support for cooperation.

Initial decision: What best improves Leo’s explanation?

Transfer: Tool losses fall after the café introduces a clear, accessible checkout system. Which conclusion is proportionate?

Field application: For a fictional shared-resource problem, identify partner, network and rule-based influences without judging anyone’s morality.

## 7. Relationships are not an ancestral instruction manual

Objective: Distinguish evolutionary models of reproduction from fixed predictions about modern individuals or ethical rules.

Initial decision: What is wrong with the universal headline?

Transfer: A survey reports an average preference difference between two sampled groups. What should be reported?

Field application: Critique a fictional relationship headline for population, measurement, effect size and the jump from description to prescription.

## 8. Culture also changes across generations

Objective: Distinguish cultural transmission from genetic inheritance and explain how the processes can interact.

Initial decision: What spread in the café example?

Transfer: A repair method spreads after a respected visitor demonstrates it, but its accuracy has not been checked. What evidence would distinguish the accounts?

Field application: Use a fictional technique to describe how it spreads, why people copy it and how usefulness could be tested separately.

## 9. A universal claim needs varied evidence

Objective: Evaluate evolutionary hypotheses using diverse samples, competing predictions and replication.

Initial decision: What is the main scope problem?

Transfer: Researchers want to test a specific adaptation account against a learning-based alternative. Which result most strengthens the hypothesis?

Field application: Draft a fictional research proposal with two competing accounts, a distinguishing prediction, sample limits and a possible disconfirming result.

## 10. An explanation is not an instruction

Objective: Build a testable evolutionary account while separating scientific description from ethical recommendation.

Initial decision: Which conclusion is invalid?

Transfer: A modern cooperation study fits several theories and has not evaluated a proposed policy. What should the article conclude?

Field application: Write a short critique of a fictional evolved-therefore-right claim, naming the empirical gap and the independent ethical question.
